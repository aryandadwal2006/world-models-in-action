# 4.2.2 V-JEPA: predicting features from video

V-JEPA extends feature prediction from images to video. In the original work, Bardes and colleagues (2024) train visual representations with a feature-prediction objective on video, without a pretrained image encoder, text, negative examples, pixel reconstruction or other external supervision. The central change from I-JEPA is the temporal structure of the input and target.

A clip can be represented as a grid of spatiotemporal tokens. The context contains visible portions of the clip, while the target contains masked spatiotemporal regions. A context encoder processes visible tokens. A target encoder produces features for hidden regions. A predictor estimates those features using context information and the target's spatial and temporal location.

An individual image contains appearance and spatial arrangement at one instant. A sequence contains change. An object at one location may be moving, stationary or about to be occluded. These possibilities can look similar in one frame but imply different future observations. Video prediction can therefore produce features sensitive to appearance and motion.

The target is not necessarily a later frame. V-JEPA's masked spatiotemporal prediction objective withholds regions across space and time. The model predicts features for those regions rather than reconstructing every pixel. The target representation defines the level of abstraction at which prediction is rewarded.

## The original empirical evidence

The original V-JEPA paper reports training on approximately two million videos collected from public datasets. Its largest model, a ViT-H/16 trained only on videos, obtains 81.9% on Kinetics-400, 72.2% on Something-Something-v2 and 77.9% on ImageNet-1K with a frozen backbone under the paper's protocols. These results show transfer to both motion-centric and appearance-centric evaluations.

A frozen-backbone score measures feature quality under a particular readout, not the ability to plan an action sequence. V-JEPA studies representation learning. It does not automatically infer the causal effect of a robot command: passive video records observed motion but does not necessarily expose the controls that produced it.

Schematically, passive video prediction uses context such as (z_{\leq t}), whereas action-conditioned prediction also receives (a_t):
[
p(z_{t+1}\mid z_{\leq t})\quad\text{versus}\quad
p(z_{t+1}\mid z_{\leq t},a_t).
]
These expressions identify the information difference; they are not literal definitions of the V-JEPA or V-JEPA 2 objectives.
