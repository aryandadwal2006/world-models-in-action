# Figure 3.1: The representation contract

**Status:** Detailed production brief only. No artwork is included in this branch.

## Purpose

Remind the reader of Chapter 2's state contract and distinguish it from the proxy relation a self-supervised loss directly constrains.

## Composition

Two panels. The first shows nuisance variants that may appropriately share a representation. The second shows a possible failure: two histories that differ in a target-relevant way are merged by an over-invariant encoder. A small label separates the desired predictive contract from the training proxy.

## Required content

Show nuisance appearance changes mapping to one representation when they do not change the target distribution. In the failure panel, two histories with different hidden velocity or contact states map to the same `z), despite different conditional futures. Label this a possible aliasing failure, not a universal statement about single frames.

## Exclusions

No latent coordinate is presumed to equal a physical variable; no claim a single frame can never contain velocity cues; no claim that the diagram introduces a new taxonomy or theorem.

## Acceptance checks

The labels "nuisance variation", "target-relevant distinction", "proxy objective" and "predictive contract" are distinct. The figure acts as a reminder of prior chapters, not a full reteaching of the state model.
